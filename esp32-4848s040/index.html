    ESP32-4848S040: notes and measurements¶

    ESP32-S3 (240 MHz), 16 MB QIO flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, 4" 480x480 ST7701 panel on a 16-bit RGB bus, GT911 touch, CH340 on UART0. MicroPython 1.29, LVGL 9.5. This is the first board of the repo on omnipython-lvgl, the successor of the archived lvgl-micropython (the STM32 and RP2350 boards followed on 2026-11-10).

    Build and bench¶

    • scripts/build.sh esp32 ESP32_4848S040, which hands over to scripts/build_esp32.sh: pins MicroPython, omnipython-lvgl (official, not a fork) and ESP-IDF v5.5.2. MicroPython 1.29 supports the 5.x series only, not ESP-IDF 6.
    • Flash, at offset 0 (the image is complete): esptool.py --chip esp32s3 -p /dev/ttyUSB0 -b 460800 write_flash 0x0 dist/<file>.bin. On a board that had other firmware run esptool.py --chip esp32s3 -p /dev/ttyUSB0 erase_flash first, which erases everything: without it the board can stop at boot with "The filesystem appears to be corrupted".
    • Run tests through the CH340: scripts/uart_run.py <test> --dtr 0 --boot-wait 5. DTR high would hold IO0 low. No native USB (see below).
    • After changing sdkconfig.board or manifest.py, delete .work/esp32/micropython/ports/esp32/build-ESP32_4848S040: an existing build keeps its old sdkconfig, and a failed build its old frozen_content.c, with no warning.
    • A global pip index that does not answer makes every ESP-IDF pip install crawl: the script sets PIP_CONFIG_FILE=/dev/null unless told otherwise.

    What this board hides¶

    • Native USB must be off. The USB PHY holds GPIO 19 (touch SDA) and 20 (panel G1). MicroPython turns it on by default on the S3, and the only symptom is an empty I2C scan: the bus has its pull-ups, SCL works, SDA cannot go low. MICROPY_HW_ENABLE_USBDEV=0.
    • Red and blue are swapped against the published pin lists: GPIO 4, 5, 6, 7, 15 are the high bits of the pixel, not the low ones. Checked by eye with colour bars and ramps.
    • The panel has no reset line: it keeps its state across a reset of the ESP32, so a change to its init does not show until the next power cycle unless the sequence starts with a software reset (0x01). It does now.
    • Not inverted. The source of the init sequence sends 0x21; here that shows the negative, so the sequence ends with 0x20.
    • GT911 RST and INT are not wired; gt911.GT911(rst=None). Coordinates are panel pixels, no rotation or mirroring (5 targets hit, 1-18 px off = finger size). read() 227 us. The page we took the pins from says SCL is 20 in the text, 45 in its JSON: it is 45.

    Display driver (modules/esp_rgb)¶

    Same interface as st_ltdc and rgbpanel, on ESP-IDF's esp_lcd. Two framebuffers in PSRAM, outside the GC heap, so the image survives a soft reset. Two bounce buffers of 20 lines in internal RAM, filled by the CPU in an interrupt: the LCD DMA reading PSRAM directly loses pixels when anything else uses that memory. That interrupt copies 24 MB/s and stays on the core that creates the panel, so esp_rgb.init() creates it from a task on the other core: MicroPython keeps its core (loop speed 137 000 -> 145 000/s of 147 000). CONFIG_LCD_RGB_ISR_IRAM_SAFE and CONFIG_SPIRAM_XIP_FROM_PSRAM keep the panel running during flash writes: code and constants run from PSRAM (which is why the heap is 5.7 MB, not 8).

    esp_lcd does not report FIFO underruns, so unlike the other drivers there is no counter: lost pixels only show on the panel.

    Drive strength of the data lines. At ESP-IDF's default drive (2, about 20 mA) the panel showed coloured specks wherever many bits change from one pixel to the next: single-pixel lines at a quarter, a half and three quarters of a grey ramp, red and green sparkles along the edges of photos. With the data lines at drive 1 (about 10 mA) or 0 the ramp and the photos are clean; the pixel clock at 10 MHz instead of 12, or data on the other clock edge, changed nothing. esp_rgb.init() sets the 16 data lines to MICROPY_HW_RGB_DATA_DRIVE, drive 1 unless the board says otherwise; the pixel clock keeps the default. Checked by eye on one board (dcb4d90487c0), with a grey ramp, the primaries and photos, 21 November 2026.

    Soft reset: this port has no board hook after a soft reset, where the other drivers call mp_lv_deinit_gc(), and a later lv.init() panics (LoadProhibited). The board's _boot.py calls esp_rgb.lvgl_guard(), which leaves an object whose finaliser does it, run by gc_sweep_all(). So no patch to MicroPython. Because a board can freeze only one _boot.py, manifest.py lists the port's modules one by one.

    Measured¶
    Panel 42.20 fps (nominal 42.27 at 12 MHz), the same during 200 KB of flash writes
    Full refresh, 48 buttons 88 ms after a pause, 94-97 ms back to back
    Small label change 2.9 ms after a pause
    gc.collect() with 2 MB of data 120 ms
    Soft resets with LVGL left initialised 3/3
    WiFi see below

    Soak of 15 minutes (tests/hil/esp32/t05_soak.py: a full-height bar, a spinner, a label, Python allocations, 2 MB of application data, gc.collect() every 10 s): LVGL 39.1 refreshes/s, panel 42.28 fps, 0 errors, 0 touch errors, memory flat (2.55-2.57 MB free). Log: docs/log/esp32-phase5-soak-4848S040.txt. gc.collect() still stops the animation for 120 ms, about 5 frames.

    Where the speed came from. Full refresh, step by step:
    panel interrupt on the same core, 16 KB/32 KB caches 145 ms
    interrupt on the other core 133 ms
    instruction cache 32 KB, data cache 64 KB, 64-byte lines 88 ms

    Everything LVGL touches is behind the cache: code and constants (XIP from PSRAM), the heap and the framebuffers. The caches cost 16 KB of internal RAM. The firmware was already -O2.

    Tried and dropped: PARTIAL mode with render buffers in internal RAM (strips copied to the hidden framebuffer, then to the other one). Worse with any height: 112-114 ms at 100 lines against 88, 290 ms at 20 before the caches: each strip walks all the objects again, and the copies add up. Rendering into PSRAM was not the limit.

    LVGL's memory pools¶

    The pools of lv_mem_core_micropython.c keep LVGL's allocations in TLSF pools held in GC blocks, so they do not walk the GC's allocation table. Since omnipython-lvgl#15 they are opt-in on ESP32 as on the other ports; this board turns them on with LVMP_POOL_TLSF in mpconfigboard.h. LVMP_POOLS=none scripts/build_esp32.sh ESP32_4848S040 builds without them, for comparisons (output -nopool). The first pool is 32 KB and each new one doubles, up to 256 KB.

    Measured with two builds of one tree (log docs/log/esp32-pools-c34fbee-4848S040.txt), with the layout script of omnipython-lvgl PR #6. The states are a fresh heap, one free block below 2 MB of bytearrays, and many small holes among them:

    no pools pools
    full refresh, median 70.8-70.9 / 471.0 / 448.7-448.9 ms 70.8-70.9 / 70.9 / 70.9 ms
    full refresh, worst of 20 71.5 / 471.3 / 472.7 ms 71.4-71.8 / 74.9-75.4 / 71.2-71.3 ms
    50 labels created and deleted 30.5 / 70.2 / 230.5 ms 29.0-36.2 / 29.4 / 33.0-34.4 ms
    gc.collect() 3.1 / 127.1 / 109.9 ms 6.8 / 123.2 / 111.3-111.4 ms
    GC heap free after display_driver 4.63 MB 4.61 MB

    On a heap that has been in use, the pools keep the refresh at its fresh-heap time instead of 6-7x slower. They cost 25 KB of heap and 3.7 ms more per collection on a fresh heap; the 15 minute soak (no holes) runs at 48.9 LVGL refreshes/s with them and 47.6 without. Audio, WiFi and the soak pass with both, 0 errors, memory flat.

    Before #15 (v9.5.53, one 256 KB pool, on multi_heap or TLSF: log docs/log/esp32-pools-4848S040.txt) the refresh was the same, but the pools cost 250 KB of heap and 13 ms per collection, and the soak ran at 43.3 refreshes/s against 47.7. From v9.5.53 to c34fbee the firmware grew by 55 KB (the LVGL assert handler of #13 kept file and function names). With CONFIG_SPIRAM_XIP_FROM_PSRAM code and constants are copied to PSRAM, each rounded up to 64 KB pages that the heap does not get: here the constants went from 10 to 11 pages, so the PSRAM region lost 64 KB (the 128 KB written here before was the free GC heap on the other board). omnipython-lvgl 665dec5 (#19: the handler gets only the file's base name and the line) is 50 KB smaller than c34fbee and back to 10 pages: the region is the v9.5.53 size again and gc.mem_free() is 64 KB higher (log docs/log/esp32-psram-665dec5.txt).

    The schedule queue¶

    MicroPython's schedule queue has 8 places, and a time.sleep_ms() drains it slowly: with a periodic 5 ms timer that schedules a second callback, as display_driver had, a 2 s sleep ran 205 ticks instead of 400. The queue stays full, and whatever else asks for a place is dropped with no error. That was the cause of two problems:

    • the I2S callback of audio.py was lost, and the sound never ended (20 sounds in 24 during long sleeps, none with polling or with the timer stopped);
    • LVGL ran below its rate while sleeping, and the gaps during downloads reached 1 s.

    display_driver now uses a one-shot timer, armed again at the end of each lv.timer_handler(), so it holds at most one place. 0 lost callbacks in 72 sounds; t05_perf.py animation from 47 to 55 refreshes/s; soak of 15 minutes 47.6 refreshes/s against 39.1, 42.28 fps, 0 errors, 0 touch errors, memory flat (docs/log/esp32-soak-oneshot-timer-4848S040.txt).

    Note: repr() of a machine.Timer on the ESP32 prints the two modes swapped.

    Sound¶

    Speaker connector, through an NS4168 on I2S: BCLK GPIO 1, LRCLK GPIO 2, DIN GPIO 40 (pins from a schematic extract on the Home Assistant community, confirmed by ear). These are also the relay pins: audio.py and buzzer.py must not be used on a board with the relays fitted.

    audio.py plays beeps, tones and WAV files (PCM, 16 bit, mono or stereo) in the background; buzzer.py goes through it, so the games have sound with no changes. With the bar animating, calls return in 2-12 ms and LVGL keeps its rate, longest gap 25 ms.

    • A sound comes out about 150 ms after the call, the time to cross the buffers.
    • The I2S must keep running until the buffers have played. With one block of silence after the sound, a 60 ms beep was never heard; with two, it is.
    • The amplifier plays one channel: a stereo file loses the other one.

    State¶

    All 10 examples start without exceptions; Breakout played by hand. Again on omnipython-lvgl c34fbee with the pools (docs/log/esp32-examples-c34fbee-4848S040.txt). On 665dec5 (2026-11-11, second board, docs/log/opl-665dec5-all-boards.txt): panel 42.20 fps, full refresh 93.7 ms on average, LVGL 54.2 refreshes/s animating, and display.set_tile_cnt(256) raises RuntimeError: LVGL assert failed at lv_display.c:614 with LVGL still running. Backlight: PWM on GPIO 38 at 2 kHz, 70% at start, dims at every step down to 10% by eye; display_driver.backlight() drives it from Python, so esp_rgb.backlight() is not the one to use. Below about 30% the panel can no longer be read.

    SD card: machine.SDCard(slot=2, sck=48, miso=41, mosi=47, cs=42, freq=10_000_000), after import display_driver: SCK and MOSI are shared with the panel's SPI, which only the start uses. Saving the settings of examples/weather.py tested by hand on a board with a card; no card on the bench board.

    Not done: the relays (GPIO 40, 2, 1).
